It is in production and has its first municipality onboarded.

Why this product wins with its buyer

The insight the product is built on is that you do not sell a mayor an app for residents — you sell the mayor their own control panel. Every Chilean mayor is legally obliged under Ley 18.695 to deliver an annual public account of their administration, and today that document is assembled by hand from spreadsheets collected from each municipal directorate. TuMuniAquí already holds the underlying data — every report, who fixed it, how long it took, what it cost, and the before/after photograph — and produces the report in one click.

That reframing is what closed the first customer: the platform was accepted on a live demonstration of the working system, with a resident report filed from a phone in the room and the WhatsApp confirmation arriving in front of the mayor.

The compounding asset

Every resolved incident writes a permanent, photographed, costed and time-stamped record into the municipality's own history. The longer a municipality uses the platform, the more valuable its public account becomes and the more expensive it is to leave. Retention is structural, not contractual.

Section 2

The problem and the wedge

In a Chilean comuna, a resident complaint arrives by WhatsApp to a councillor, on Facebook, at the front desk, or verbally to the mayor in the street. Three costs follow from that, and every one of them is felt politically.

No record exists

Nobody knows how many complaints are open, which ones repeat, or which has gone three weeks untouched. Work is prioritised by whoever complained loudest most recently.

The resident never hears back

Even when the municipality fixes the problem, the resident is not told. The work gets done and is not seen — which is politically identical to not doing it.

The public account is manual

Each directorate sends a spreadsheet, someone merges them at the last minute, and the figures come from memory rather than from a register.

What the platform converts each complaint into

A ticket with a number, an owner, a department, a severity, a location on a map, a budget, an actual cost, a resolution time, photographic evidence before and after, and a resident satisfaction rating. That single conversion is the whole product.

The addressable market

DimensionDetail
ChileMore than 340 comunas, each an autonomous buyer with its own budget, its own mayor and its own legal obligation to render an annual public account.
Beachhead segmentSmall and mid-sized comunas — under-served by enterprise GovTech vendors, and precisely the ones for whom the Compra Ágil purchase route removes the tender barrier.
Buying triggerNon-discretionary and calendar-driven: the annual public account is required by law every year, for every mayor, forever.
Adjacent expansionThe product is not Chile-specific in its mechanics. Any country with municipal service requests and a mandated accountability report is a port, not a rebuild — the tenant model, categories, departments and legal text are configuration.
Rural design as a differentiator

The platform was built for the Chilean comuna as it actually is, not for a capital city. Addresses are often verbal ("past the bridge, opposite the school"), mobile coverage is patchy, and street-view mapping does not exist. So the product ships free-text location references, an offline queue that submits the report when signal returns, a satellite map layer using Esri World Imagery (no API key, no billing account), and a locality-based sector model built from a centre point and a radius rather than GeoJSON polygons a small municipality could never produce. Enterprise competitors do not build for this user.

How it works, end to end

The core loop runs from the resident's phone to the mayor's annual report without anyone re-keying data. Each stage below is live in production today.

Three steps on the phone: location, category, photo. No download, no signup.

2 · System triages

→
3 · Municipality acts
Department head budgets, names the crew, assigns. Field crew closes with photo and actual cost.
→
4 · Proof returns
Resident gets a WhatsApp and rates the service. The mayor's public account writes itself.
The resident never logs in. The municipality never exports a spreadsheet.

Stage 1 — the resident files a report

A resident opens the municipality's own URL (for example /licanten), which loads that tenant's name, logo and colours. The form is a three-step wizard designed for one hand, in sunlight, by an older adult:

StepWhat is askedHow it is made easy
1Location GPS by default, or tap the map. An address search runs on Nominatim (OpenStreetMap) — free, no API key. A satellite toggle lets rural residents recognise a place with no street names.
2Category and description A custom bottom-sheet picker with a search box that ignores accents ("arbol" finds "Árbol caído"), across 58 categories in 9 colour-coded groups. The resident never has to know which municipal directorate handles it.
3Photo and contact Up to three photos, compressed in the browser before upload. Name and a validated Chilean WhatsApp number, stored normalised as +569XXXXXXXX so the notification layer can use it directly.

Before the report is created, the app checks for an existing active report of the same category within 50 metres (Haversine distance against the live public ticket feed). If one exists, the resident is shown it and offered "add me to this report" instead of creating a duplicate — so the municipality sees one problem with twenty residents behind it, not twenty problems.

If the phone has no signal, the report is queued in local storage and submitted automatically when connectivity returns. Writes are idempotent — the document ID and ticket number are generated client-side and reused on retry — so a flaky connection can never produce a duplicate record.

Stage 2 — the system classifies before a human sees it

Severity triage
Every category maps to High, Medium or Low. Gas leaks, exposed cabling, sinkholes, fallen trees and dead street lights are High; potholes and blocked drains Medium; graffiti and overgrown grass Low. No resident input, so no gaming.
Department routing
The same category maps to one of 7 municipal directorates — Obras (DOM), Tránsito, Operaciones, Aseo y Ornato, Medio Ambiente, Seguridad Ciudadana, Oficina de Partes — so the report lands on the right desk with no triage meeting.
Sector assignment
Coordinates resolve to a named locality using a centre-plus-radius model; where circles overlap, the nearest centre wins. This is what lets a mayor ask "which sector am I neglecting?"
Public ticket
A privacy-safe projection of the report is written to a separate collection so anyone can check its status without a login — and without the resident's name, contact or ID number ever being exposed.

Stage 3 — the municipality works the case

The department head sees only their own department's reports. Assigning a crew opens a budgeting modal: they tick the specific workers from their roster (each worker carries their own hourly rate), enter estimated hours and a materials description, and the labour cost is computed automatically. Only then does the ticket move to In Progress — the state change and the budget are a single act, so no work is ever in progress without a number attached to it.

The field crew view lists what is in progress, and closing a case requires a photo of the finished work plus actual hours and final materials cost. Those two mandatory fields are what make the mayor's monthly spend figure real rather than notional.

Stage 4 — the proof goes back out

The resident receives a WhatsApp when the report is received and again when it is resolved, from an official Meta Cloud API number. They can check status at any time without logging in, add a follow-up comment or photo to an existing report, and rate the service one to five stars once it is closed. Those ratings roll up into a "resident satisfaction" indicator on the mayor's panel — and into the annual public account.

A design rule worth noting

Section 5

The WhatsApp layer

WhatsApp is the default communication channel in Chile, and it is where this product closes the loop with the resident. The integration runs on Meta's official Cloud API — not on an unofficial automation library — which matters more to an acquirer than any feature in it.

Why the official API is a material asset

Unofficial WhatsApp automation (Baileys, whatsapp-web.js and similar) violates Meta's terms of service, and Meta bans the number without warning or appeal. A GovTech product whose notification channel can vanish overnight is not sellable to a municipality. TuMuniAquí was migrated off that approach onto the official Graph API with an approved business number, approved message templates, and a system-user access token that does not expire. That migration is done, verified in production, and is a permanent de-risking of the asset.

What is live today

EventMechanismBehaviour
Report received Approved template alerta_nuevo_ticket The resident is messaged within moments of filing, with the readable category name and their ticket number, plus a link to check status.
Report resolved Approved template ticket_resuelto The resident is told the work is done and invited to rate the service at the portal link.
Resident enquiry Signed inbound webhook A resident messaging the municipal number with a ticket number gets its current status back. Anything the bot does not understand returns a three-button tappable menu rather than an error.

How it is engineered

  • Idempotent by design. Each notification has its own boolean flag on the incident record, set only once the send succeeds. A restart, a redeploy or a duplicate event cannot double-message a resident.
  • Self-healing backlog. If Meta rejects a send — for example while a template is still under review — the flag stays false and the message is retried later. When the templates were approved, the queued notifications went out on their own.
  • Signed webhook. The inbound endpoint is never mounted without signature verification configured. A public URL that would let anyone make the platform message arbitrary numbers is refused at startup rather than exposed.
  • Server-side identity. The service authenticates to the database with the Firebase Admin SDK under its own service account — there is no shared officer login or password anywhere in the notification path.
  • Independent deployment. The notification service is a standalone Node/Express application. It can be restarted, scaled or moved without touching the web application residents use.
  • Error translation. Meta's numeric error codes are translated into plain-language diagnostics in the logs, so an operator can tell a template problem from a token problem without reading Meta's documentation.

Multi-tenant readiness of the channel

The database listeners are already tenant-aware. The message templates themselves currently name the first municipality inside their approved text — parameterising the municipality name and the portal link is a defined, documented task with the exact steps written down, and it is the one thing to do before the second municipality goes live. Meta's two-number limit per account lifts with standard business verification.

Section 6

Intelligence built into the product

Several behaviours look like small features and are actually the reason the platform survives contact with a real municipality. They are listed here because they are the parts a buyer would otherwise have to discover, and rebuild, the hard way.

Waze-style de-duplication

Five residents reporting the same pothole is the failure mode that kills citizen-reporting products: the queue fills with noise and the staff stop trusting it. TuMuniAquí detects a report of the same category within 50 metres of an existing active one and offers to merge. The support count is the political signal — twenty residents behind one report is what moves a case up the queue. Anonymous voting is permitted by the security rules only if the write touches exclusively the vote counter and the voter list, and increments each by exactly one; no other field can be altered by an unauthenticated visitor.

Automatic triage and routing

Severity and owning department are derived from the category, never entered by the resident. That removes the two most common sources of dispute — a resident marking everything urgent, and an inter-departmental argument about whose problem it is — and it means the queue is correctly ordered from the moment a report lands.

The costing module

ElementDetail
Worker rosterEach worker carries a name, role, hourly rate, department and daily attendance state — so labour cost is computed from the actual people assigned, not a flat rate.
Estimated budgetNamed workers, estimated hours, materials description and an overall estimate, captured at the moment of assignment.
Actual spendReal hours and final materials cost, mandatory at closure — the button stays disabled until both are entered.
Mayor's spend KPIMonthly total, filterable by department, with a line-by-line view showing hours, the named crew, computed labour cost and materials for each closed case.
Reaction timeMeasured from report creation to crew assignment — and deliberately written only once, so re-assigning a crew later cannot quietly improve the metric.
Metric integrity

That last row is a good example of the engineering standard in this codebase. An earlier version reset the assignment timestamp on every re-assignment, which meant a two-week-old case could be made to look freshly picked up simply by pressing a button — the indicator improved by being clicked. It was found and fixed, and the reasoning is written down. Metrics a mayor will publish have to be defensible.

The sector model

Mayors think in localities, villages and rural sectors, and the political question they ask is which sector is being neglected. Sectors are defined as a centre point and a radius rather than polygons, so a small municipality can produce them from Google Maps in minutes with no GIS tool and no GeoJSON. Where two circles overlap, the nearest centre wins; anything outside every sector is grouped explicitly as "outside defined sectors" and explained, so a gap is visible rather than silent. For the first municipality, 23 localities are loaded, with the road-adjacent settlements aligned to the actual geometry of the regional route rather than to interpolated coordinates.

Resilience details

  • Idempotent report creation. Document ID and ticket number are generated client-side and reused on retry, so a dropped connection cannot create two records.
  • Ticket uniqueness without transactions. The public ticket collection is create-only for anonymous users, so a collision with an existing number is rejected by the database itself and the number is regenerated — no locking, no contention.
  • Bounded queries. Live listeners are scoped by tenant and by category rather than reading whole collections, and degrade gracefully to a wider query if a composite index is unavailable.
  • Browser-side image compression. Photos are compressed before upload, which is what makes the product usable on a rural connection.
  • Anti-spam. A per-device cooldown limits repeat submissions from the same handset.
  • Graceful failure page. If the deployment is misconfigured, the app catches it during module load and shows an explanatory screen with emergency phone numbers visible — instead of a spinner that never stops.
Section 7

The public-accountability engine

This is the feature that converts a mayor from interested to committed, and it is the strategic core of the product's positioning.

Cuenta Pública in one click

Every mayor in Chile must deliver an annual public account under Ley 18.695. Today that document is assembled by hand. TuMuniAquí generates it from the work already recorded, for the current year, the previous year, or the trailing twelve months.

Section of the reportContent
Period summaryThe headline figure — problems resolved — with the period's totals.
How we respondedReaction time, resolution time, resident satisfaction, labour hours deployed.
Performance by directorateA table showing each municipal department's volume and outcomes.
What residents reportedTop categories and the severity distribution.
Month-by-month evolutionThe trend across the period.
Investment executedActual spend, from the closing costs recorded by the crews.

Two engineering decisions worth reading

  • No PDF library. The report is produced through the browser's own print dialogue with dedicated print stylesheets — colour preservation forced, page breaks avoided inside sections and immediately after headings. Zero additional bundle weight, and it works on any machine in the municipality.
  • Charts in pure CSS, not a charting library. Canvas and SVG charts routinely print blank or cropped. With CSS the browser prints exactly what is on screen. This is the difference between a feature that demos well and a document a mayor can actually hand out.
A detail that shows the standard

When the report was first validated against real data, reaction time displayed as −7 hours, caused by migrated records whose assignment timestamp preceded their creation. A negative number in the mayor's published document would destroy the credibility of every other figure in it. The fix was to discard incoherent intervals rather than let them distort an average — and the same correction was applied to the mayor's panel and the public transparency page, which carried the same latent risk.

The public transparency page

A public page per comuna, supporting the active-transparency obligations of Ley 20.285. Residents and councillors can see what has been reported and resolved without any login and without any personal data being exposed — the public projection of a report deliberately excludes the reporter's name, contact details and identity number.

Why this matters commercially

Most citizen-reporting products sell efficiency, which is a benefit a municipality can defer. This one sells a legally mandated deliverable that recurs every year regardless of budget climate or political priority. The buyer is not choosing whether to produce a public account — only whether to produce it by hand.

Multi-tenancy

Each municipality is a single document whose ID is its URL slug, holding its institutional name, brand colours, logo, map centre and crew names. Residents reach their municipality at /{slug}; the theme is applied at runtime through CSS custom properties.

Officials are different, and deliberately so: their routes carry no slug at all. The tenant is read from the officer's own profile record after login, never from the URL — so an official cannot cross into another municipality's data by editing the address bar. Adding a municipality is a data operation, not a deployment.

Self-service official management is built in: a mayor creates and revokes accounts from the dashboard. Roughly 400 lines of declarative Firestore and Storage security rules enforce all of the above at the data layer, so the guarantees hold against a direct API call, not only against the user interface.

Section 9

Security, privacy and Chilean law

This is the section a municipality's legal adviser reads, and it is written to survive that review. Data protection is engineered into the data model rather than asserted in a policy document.

Data minimisation, enforced structurally

  • The public projection cannot leak identity. Status lookup and the transparency page read a separate collection that never receives the reporter's name, contact details, identity number or free-text notes. The exclusion is in the write path, not in a display filter.
  • National identity numbers are no longer collected at all. The field, its validator and its lookup path were removed from the product to avoid handling sensitive personal data without necessity. Historical records that carry one retain it; nothing new is written.
  • An earlier design that would have exposed data was rejected on analysis. Looking up tickets by identity number was implemented against a device-local index rather than a server-side one, precisely because a publicly readable index would have let anyone enumerate plausible identity numbers and learn who reported what.
  • Real personal data was purged from the demonstration tenant. Identity numbers and mobile numbers belonging to real people were deleted from the demo environment, with a backup taken first.

Access control and secrets

  • Officials authenticate individually; there is no shared account anywhere in the system.
  • The notification service uses its own service-account credential, not a human login.
  • The inbound webhook refuses to mount without signature verification configured.
  • Anonymous writes are constrained by rule to exactly two operations — creating a report, and adding a single vote — and every other field is unwritable without an authenticated, authorised profile.
  • Runtime secrets live in the deployment platform's environment configuration and Google Secret Manager, never in the repository.

Operations and infrastructure

The operating burden is deliberately small. A single technical operator can run the platform for multiple municipalities, and the deployment path requires no terminal.

Hard-won reliability engineering

That pre-flight check exists because of a real failure that is worth understanding: Vite does not fail the build when a required environment variable is missing — it substitutes undefined and compiles successfully. The site then deploys green, and the resident sees a loading spinner forever with nothing in the logs. Both ends are now fixed: the pipeline refuses to build with missing configuration, and the application catches the failure during module load and renders an explanatory page with emergency numbers visible. This class of silent failure is the most expensive kind for a live public service, and it has been designed out.

  • Point-in-time backups were taken before every destructive maintenance operation performed to date, and retained.
  • Maintenance scripts are written defensively — the sector loader, for instance, refuses to write coordinates that fall outside Chile or a non-positive radius, because a silently misplaced sector is invisible on screen.

Maintenance tooling included

Sixteen operational scripts ship with the repository, covering tenant setup, sector loading and verification, demo preparation and refresh, coordinate repair, orphan-record cleanup, WhatsApp configuration, data-protection contact configuration, overdue-assignment review, and pre-presentation verification. These are the tools that make onboarding a new municipality a repeatable procedure rather than a bespoke project.

Section 12

The regulatory moat

This is the least obvious and most valuable piece of commercial design in the business, and it deserves to be understood clearly before valuing the asset.

The mechanism

Under Chilean public-procurement law (Ley 21.634), a municipality may purchase goods and services below a threshold of 100 UTM through Compra Ágil — a simplified, online, same-week purchase route. Above that threshold, the municipality must run a public tender: months of process, formal technical specifications, and open competition against established vendors with dedicated bid teams.

What the pricing does with it

The price was not derived from cost or from the value of the work. It was set by the location of the buying barrier. The Comuna plan lands at roughly 66 UTM for a full first year and the Comuna Mayor plan at roughly 96 UTM — both under the ceiling. The headroom on the entry plan is deliberate, so that a movement in the indexed units, or an additional item requested by the municipality, does not push the purchase over the line and destroy the route to market.

Why this is a moat and not just a price point

A competitor selling an enterprise GovTech platform at enterprise pricing is structurally unable to reach this buyer without a tender — and a tender for a small comuna is rarely worth an enterprise vendor's cost of bidding. TuMuniAquí can be bought in a week, by a municipality too small for the incumbents to chase, at a price that fits a line item rather than a capital programme. The pricing, the product scope and the buyer segment are aligned by design, and that alignment is the defensible position.

La ventaja regulatoria — el punto más importante del modelo

El precio no se fijó por costo ni por el valor del trabajo, sino por dónde está la barrera de compra. Bajo 100 UTM, una municipalidad puede contratar por Compra Ágil (Ley 21.634), sin licitación pública. Los planes Comuna y Comuna Mayor caben bajo ese tope, con holgura deliberada en el plan de entrada. Un competidor con precio empresarial no puede llegar a este comprador sin licitación — y una licitación de una comuna pequeña rara vez le compensa. Esa alineación entre precio, alcance y segmento es la posición defendible del negocio.

Características del ingreso

  • Sin cobro por uso: ni por reporte, ni por vecino, ni por funcionario. Un municipio que usa mucho la plataforma paga lo mismo que uno que la usa poco — y es un argumento de venta real.
  • Caja adelantada: la puesta en marcha se cobra al inicio, y el pago anual anticipado adelanta el año completo cobrando 11 meses en vez de 12.
  • Contraparte pública: las municipalidades no cambian de proveedor con facilidad; la renovación es un acto administrativo, no una decisión de compra desde cero.
  • Venta por referencia: alcaldes y directores municipales son una comunidad conectada. El primer cliente de referencia vale de forma desproporcionada, y ya existe.
